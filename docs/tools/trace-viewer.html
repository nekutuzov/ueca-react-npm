Click a row to read the whole record beside it, and to highlight every other event of the same model; click a chip under details to filter by it. The same panel opens from a dot on the Timeline or an arrow on the Sequence. The record panel carries what a column cannot: the full value, the render pass the event sat inside, the dispatch it belonged to, and the same model’s events either side of it. The table says what the panel shows; the panel says where the table is looking — clicking a related record scrolls the table to it and marks it, and leaves the panel on the record you are reading. To read a different one, click its row.
The filter box also takes field:value terms — cast:1, prop:caption, kind:render, level:error — which match a field exactly, so cast:1 does not also bring back cast #10. Hover any row for the console line it came from.
One lane per model, in the order the models first appear. Click a dot to read the whole record beside it; a dense column of render dots on one lane is a re-render storm. An application spends most of its life doing nothing, and on a plain time axis those idle stretches push all the activity into a few pixels, so every quiet stretch here is compressed to a narrow grey band labelled with the time it swallowed and every burst gets room.
The time axis always fits the pane, so what runs off the bottom is the lanes: scroll or drag to move down them, and use the overview at the corner to cross the whole trace in one movement. To look more closely at a stretch of time, narrow the scope — drag a stretch on the strip above and every view follows, this one included.
Lanes are models, keyed by identity — a model keeps its lane when its path changes. Click an arrow to read the whole record beside it. Scroll to zoom, drag to move. The diagram grows both ways — a lane for every participant, a row for every event — so it is steered rather than scrolled; the overview at the corner says where in it you are, and takes you anywhere on it.
One bus dispatch is drawn as a call and a return: a solid arrow from the component that sent the message to the one that handled it, and a dashed one back when it completes. The send is that same call seen from the caller’s end, so it is not drawn twice — it appears on its own only when nothing handled the message. Where several components answered, no single one of them completed it, so the completion stays a mark on the caller’s own lane. The other arrow is a model being cached by its owner.
Clicking a row folds or unfolds it — nothing else; scoping the other views to a component is the only and hide buttons on each row, the same two lists Components holds. The ownership hierarchy is rebuilt from the dotted paths, and the keyboard folds too: Enter, and the arrow keys.
The bar is that component's own events, coloured by kind and scaled against the busiest component in view, so a mostly-blue bar is a component that renders and a mostly-orange one is a component whose properties keep changing. The counts are its own events and, in grey, the total including everything beneath it. The hierarchy is built from the whole trace, so filtering dims components rather than making them vanish.
The application as it is wired: components nested inside their owners, the lines between them the messages and bindings that actually carried something. It lights up as the application works, while Follow is on.Press play to watch it happen. A box is a component and it sits inside the one that owns it — click a header to fold a subtree away, the same folds the Tree view uses.
The wiring reads by SIDE before it reads by colour. Every bus dispatch leaves a component's left edge, runs down the one trunk in the gutter, and comes back in at the handler's left edge; every binding hangs off a channel on its owner's right, inside the owner's own box. The two systems never meet, and where they cross they cross square.
Three marks say what an end is. A ring is an origin — the signal leaves here. A dot is a junction, where a branch taps a line that carries on past it. An arrowhead is a destination. A binding branch with an arrowhead at both ends is a two-way bond: either end can drive it. Two rings on one line would mean nobody receives, so it cannot happen.
Point at a ring or an arrowhead to follow that one route end to end while the rest recede — it keeps its own colour and gains a halo, because a highlight that changed hue would make a lit bus route read as a binding; point at the trunk itself to light everything running through it. Nothing is dashed, because a line is a connection that exists continuously — what moves is the message, and while one is in flight it runs along its route as marks punched out of the page. A box's border lights in the colour of whatever it just did, and the component the play head is standing on keeps a yellow ring until the head moves on.
The picture opens short: any part of the tree that never touches the bus is folded when the trace loads, and within a container only the components that talk get a full-width row — the quiet ones sit in columns beside each other, several to a row.
A container is as tall as what it holds, and a folded one is as tall as the wiring that crosses into it — its children's ports float up to its own edges and no line disappears. So a tall folded box means "much of the application reaches in here" and a short one means "self-contained". There are no counts on the picture: nearly every trace is one cascade, and a per-component total measures which cascade touched it rather than how busy it is. The record panel is where quantity lives.
Scroll to move, Ctrl+scroll to zoom. There is no scrollbar: the overview in the corner says where you are and how much there is, and it says it better than a trough on one edge could. The picture only ever grows downward, so it scrolls like a document, and it opens at life size — the sizes it is drawn at were chosen to be read at 100%. − and + step through the usual round percentages, the number between them is the way back to 100%, and width and all fit it to the pane. Neither the scroll nor the zoom is touched by a refresh, so a live trace can grow underneath a view you have set without moving it.
The whole application is always drawn, so the picture does not rearrange itself as you filter — the filter decides what lights up, not what exists, and a component with nothing in scope sits quiet. Everything else on the page still applies: the kinds, the components, the text, and the stretch of time the strip is scoped to.
